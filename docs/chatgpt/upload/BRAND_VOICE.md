# Brand Voice & Messaging

## Tone [APPROVED — Bible slide 9]

**Direct. Clear. Positive. Action-first.**
- Use short sentences.
- Lead with the answer, then the explanation.
- Use numbers and evidence when making performance claims.
- Sound commercially aware, not overly technical.
- Avoid inflated language unless it is objectively supported.

## Writing rules [RECOMMENDATION]

**Do**
- Lead with the outcome: "More qualified leads at a lower cost per lead," not "We run Meta campaigns."
- Use one idea per sentence and one message per section.
- Use specific verbs: plan, launch, test, cut, scale, prove.
- Write in sentence case in English. Use caps only for short labels and eyebrows.

**Don't**
- Use filler like "passionate about creativity", "innovative solutions", "one-stop shop", "cutting-edge", "synergy", "360°", "next-level".
- Use superlatives without proof: "best", "leading", "No. 1", "top agency in KSA".
- Promise guaranteed results, guaranteed ROAS, or "viral".
- Use jargon chains ("omnichannel full-funnel growth-hacking solutions").

## Claims policy [RECOMMENDATION — mandatory once approved]

1. Every number published names its **source, client, period, and baseline** internally, and is approved for publication by the client where it identifies them.
2. Results from work done **before SayTamm existed** are labelled as leadership/team experience, never as SayTamm results (see `PORTFOLIO_AND_CASE_STUDIES.md`).
3. Market statistics need a named, dated source.
4. Unverified figures in drafts are marked `[[NEEDS APPROVAL]]` and must never ship.
5. No testimonial is published without written client consent and an attributable name or role.

## Arabic voice [RECOMMENDATION]

- Use **Modern Standard Arabic with a light Saudi register**, clear and business-like. Use colloquial forms only for brand signatures (قول تم) and campaign headlines.
- Write Arabic copy *from the brief*, not from the English copy. Lengths and structures may differ, and that is fine.
- Prefer Arabic terms buyers actually search and use. Keep accepted English terms where Saudi marketers use them (ROAS, CPL, SEO), and give the Arabic gloss on first use.
- Use Western Arabic numerals (0–9) for KPIs and data **[ASSUMPTION: common in Saudi business contexts; confirm, OQ-17]**.
- Never machine-translate. Every Arabic string gets native review before launch.
